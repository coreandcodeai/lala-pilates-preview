Is reformer Pilates good for back pain?

By Ellie · Founder & Instructor, La La Pilates Glasgow · STOTT® trained

Short answer: for many people, yes. Reformer Pilates builds the deep core and postural muscles that support the spine, using spring-supported, low-impact movement — which is why physiotherapists often recommend it for ongoing, non-specific back pain. It is not a treatment for injury, though: if your pain is new, severe or radiating, see a GP or physiotherapist before booking any class.

Why the reformer suits sore backs

Most back-friendly exercise has the same problem: the moves that build strength can also be the moves that hurt. The reformer softens that trade-off in three ways.

  • The springs support you. Unlike mat work, where your body carries all the load, the reformer's springs can take some of it — so you can strengthen a movement pattern before you fully load it.
  • Much of the work happens lying down. Footwork, bridging and many core exercises are done on your back with your spine supported by the carriage, which most sore backs tolerate well.
  • It's low-impact and controlled. No jumping, no jarring — resistance comes from smooth spring tension, and every exercise moves at your pace.

What a 4-person studio changes

Adaptation only works if your instructor actually sees you. At La La Pilates every class is capped at 4 people, so when you tell me your lower back grumbles, I'm watching your positioning on every exercise — changing the spring, the range or the exercise itself the moment something doesn't look or feel right. In a class of twenty, that's simply not possible.

Several of our members first came through a physiotherapist's recommendation, continuing their strength work after finishing treatment. If you're in that position, bring your physio's guidance with you — I'll build around it.

When to see a professional first

Reformer Pilates is exercise, not healthcare. Book a GP or physiotherapist appointment before class if your pain is new and severe, follows an accident, radiates down a leg, or comes with numbness, tingling or weakness. Once you've been assessed, we can carry the strengthening work forward safely — often starting with a private session where everything is tailored to you.

Where to start

If your back is generally grumbly rather than injured, start with a Beginner Group Class — slower pace, full setup help, 4 people maximum. Mention your back when you book on Momence and arrive ten minutes early so we can talk it through. The £60 Intro Offer gives you 3 sessions to feel the difference.

Can I do reformer Pilates with sciatica?

Often yes, with adaptations — but tell us when you book, and if the pain is new, severe or radiating, see your GP or physiotherapist first. A private session is usually the best starting point.

How long until my back feels stronger?

It varies, but think weeks of consistency rather than days — reformer Pilates is gradual strengthening, not an instant fix. Most people notice posture and awareness changes first, then strength.
